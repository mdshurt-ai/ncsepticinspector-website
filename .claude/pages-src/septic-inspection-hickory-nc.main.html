Public water doesn't mean public sewer

Hickory runs its own water and sewer utilities, and the city's water system also supplies Maiden, Long View and other nearby areas. That's the trap: a house on city water can still be on a septic tank, especially outside the city limits and in neighborhoods that were built before sewer lines reached them. Confirm how a property handles wastewater before you assume, because a standard home inspection won't tell you, and the answer decides whether a septic inspection belongs on the order.

Where septic is in use, the systems around Hickory range from 1990s-era precast tanks in subdivisions to much older installations on the county roads. Census estimates put Hickory's median year built at 1982, newer than Morganton or Lenoir but with plenty of houses from the 1960s and 1970s whose tanks have never been opened.

Lake Hickory and Lookout Shoals

Lake Hickory forms the city's northern edge, and lake properties bring two septic questions: how close the drain field sits to the water, and how a steep lot was graded to fit a system at all. Downstream, Helene pushed Lookout Shoals Lake more than nine feet above full pool in September 2024, the highest since 1940, and Catawba County counted about a hundred flooded homes in that area. A system that was under water needs to be looked at for settling, shifted components and effluent surfacing, and I'll say so in the report if I see them.

Which county has the permit

Most of Hickory is in Catawba County, so most records are with Catawba County Environmental Health in Newton, which takes online requests for a copy of an existing permit. Addresses west of town may be in Burke County (Burke Environmental Health, Morganton) and north of the river in Caldwell County (Caldwell Environmental Health, Lenoir). The county tax record for the parcel settles which one. Office details for all three.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Hickory septic FAQ

My Hickory house is on city water. Could it still have a septic tank?

Yes. City water and city sewer are separate questions, and plenty of homes around Hickory are on public water with a private septic system. Check the tax record, the disclosure statement, or look for a cleanout and a tank lid in the yard, and when in doubt, ask.

Which county holds the septic permit for a Hickory property?

It depends on the parcel. Most of Hickory is in Catawba County; parts of the city reach into Burke and Caldwell counties. The county listed on the tax record is the one whose environmental health office has the permit.

Do you inspect lake houses on Lake Hickory?

Yes. Lake lots often have tight setbacks and steep grades, so mention it when you book. If the property flooded during Helene, say that too, and I'll give the drain field area extra attention.
