Septic Inspections in Taylorsville, NC

Septic has always been the norm in Alexander County. From downtown Taylorsville to the farms and homesteads in the Brushy Mountains foothills and the lake homes near Lake Hickory, most properties here depend on a tank and a drain field, and I inspect them.

Where septic is nine homes in ten

The most recent county-level count of how homes handled wastewater, from 1990, showed roughly 88% of housing in the Catawba basin part of Alexander County on septic and about 91% in the Yadkin basin part. Sewer service is still limited: the Town of Taylorsville serves the central and eastern county and the City of Hickory serves Bethlehem. Public water reaches more of the county than sewer does, so a house on county water is very likely still on septic.

Taylorsville's median year built is 1976, about 30% of homes in town were built before 1960, and manufactured homes make up nearly a quarter of the county's housing. Older systems with no record, homemade tanks, and manufactured-home systems outgrown by additions are the recurring themes, and the inspection is built for them.

Farms, orchards and lake homes

About two-thirds of the county is farmland, and rural systems bring their own questions: a drain field under a pasture that's had equipment driven across it, a tank that's been buried deeper by grading, or a system that served a farmhouse for decades before the current addition went on. Lake homes near Lake Hickory and the Catawba River bring the usual setback and slope questions. Either way, the inspection starts by finding the tank, and on an older rural lot that can be the longest part of the day.

Alexander County septic records

Septic permits in the county go through the Alexander County Health Department's Environmental Health division, 151 W Main Ave. in Taylorsville, 828-632-1000. There's no online search; call or visit to ask for the permit and any layout on file. The office also evaluates existing systems for additions and replacements, which is the conversation to have if the permit's bedroom count doesn't match the house. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Taylorsville septic FAQ

Do you inspect septic systems on farms and large rural lots?

Yes. Expect the locating step to take longer on a large lot with no layout drawing, and tell me if equipment has been driven over the drain field area; that's one of the things the inspection looks at.

How do I get the Alexander County septic permit?

Call or visit Alexander County Environmental Health at 151 W Main Ave., Taylorsville, 828-632-1000. There's no online search. Ask for the permit, any layout drawing and the soil evaluation.

Do you serve Bethlehem, Stony Point and Hiddenite?

Yes. All of Alexander County is in my service area, including the Bethlehem community on Lake Hickory.
