Septic in Caldwell County

The last county-wide count of how homes handled wastewater, the 1990 Census, put close to two-thirds of the homes in the Catawba basin part of Caldwell County on septic, and nearly nine in ten in the smaller Yadkin basin area to the north. Lenoir's housing is older than most of the towns I serve, with a median year built of 1972 and more than a third of homes dating from before 1960, so a lot of those systems are forty or fifty years old. That's past the 15-to-40-year range EPA gives as a typical septic lifespan.

What that means in practice: tanks with no permit on file, homemade tanks of block or poured concrete, inlet and outlet pipes that have cracked or corroded, and drain fields that were sized for a smaller house than the one standing there now. The inspection is built to find exactly those things.

After Helene along Wilson Creek

The National Weather Service described Caldwell County's Helene flooding as likely the worst in its recorded history, with Collettsville the hardest-hit community. A septic system that sat under moving water can come out of it with a settled tank, lines knocked out of position or a drain field full of silt. On any property in those areas, I look for the warning signs NCDHHS listed after the storm and I report what I find, including anything that limited the inspection.

Caldwell County septic records

Caldwell County Environmental Health, 2345 Morganton Blvd. in Lenoir (828-426-8579, envhealth@caldwellcountync.org), keeps the septic permits and takes requests on its Septic System Record Request form. Ask for the permit, the layout and the soil evaluation; the permit's bedroom count is the number the state's inspection rule asks me to compare with the house. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Lenoir septic FAQ

The house is old and nobody knows if there's a permit. Can you still inspect it?

Yes. Many Caldwell County systems predate the county's files. The inspection then starts from the ground: I locate the tank, open it, evaluate what's there, and the report states what records were available and what wasn't.

Do you cover Collettsville, Patterson and the Wilson Creek area?

Yes. If the property flooded during Helene, tell me when you book so I can plan extra time for the drain field area and for finding a tank that may have shifted or been buried deeper by silt.

How do I get the Caldwell County septic permit?

Use the Septic System Record Request form on Caldwell County Environmental Health's septic and wells page, or call 828-426-8579. Ask for the permit, the layout drawing and the soil evaluation.
