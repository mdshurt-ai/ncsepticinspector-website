Newer houses, same questions

The City of Conover provides water and sewer inside its limits, and much of the city's housing went up in the 1990s and 2000s. Outside the lines, septic systems from that era are mostly precast concrete tanks with proper permits and layouts on file, which makes them easier to find and compare with the house. Easier to find isn't the same as fine: a thirty-year-old tank can have a failed outlet baffle, a crushed line from a vehicle driven over the field, or roots in the inlet, and the drain field may have been sized for a three-bedroom house that now has four.

About one in ten Conover homes is a manufactured home. Their systems are permitted for the original unit, and additions, decks and converted rooms are where the bedroom count and the permit tend to part ways.

Catawba County septic records

Conover's septic permits are kept by Catawba County Environmental Health in Newton, which takes online requests for a copy of an existing permit and can be reached at EHAdmin@catawbacountync.gov or through the county at 828-465-8200. For a 1990s system the permit and layout are usually on file and worth pulling before the inspection. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Conover septic FAQ

Does a 1990s septic system still need an inspection?

Yes. Age is only one factor. Baffles, tees, filters and lines fail on their own schedule, roots don't care what year a tank was poured, and the bedroom count on the permit may not match the house today.

Do you inspect manufactured-home septic systems in Conover?

Yes. The first thing I compare is the permit's bedroom count with the home as it stands, additions included, because that's where manufactured-home systems most often fall short.

Where are Conover's septic permits?

With Catawba County Environmental Health in Newton. Request a copy of an existing permit online through the county's Environmental Health forms page, or email EHAdmin@catawbacountync.gov.
