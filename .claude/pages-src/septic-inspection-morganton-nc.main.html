Septic around Morganton

Inside the city, most homes are on municipal sewer. Step outside the limits toward Lake James, Lake Rhodhiss, Salem, Drexel or the South Mountains and septic becomes the norm. The last count that asked how homes handled wastewater was the 1990 Census, and at that time more than half of the homes in the Catawba basin part of Burke County were on septic. The share has shifted since, but a large part of the county still relies on a tank and a drain field.

Age is the other factor. Census estimates put Morganton's median home at 1976, with more than half the city's houses built before 1980, and the rural housing stock skews older still. Systems from that era were often permitted on a card, installed with a homemade or early precast tank, and never opened again. I've dug up tanks around Morganton that were built by hand out of block and poured concrete, with lids cracked clean through.

Lake lots and the river

Lake James and Lake Rhodhiss lots deserve a careful look at the drain field. Setbacks from the water, steep grades down to the shoreline, and seasonal second-home use all affect how a system performs, and a field that looks fine in a dry August can tell a different story in March. Helene put Lake James at its highest level on record in September 2024 and pushed the Catawba River over Green Street in town; on any property that took water, NCDHHS's advice is to have the septic system inspected, and I look for settling over the tank and field, components that shifted, and effluent on the surface.

Burke County septic records

Burke County Environmental Health, at 200 Avery Avenue in Morganton (828-764-9000), issues septic permits, repair permits and existing-system verifications, and it keeps the permit and layout records. The county posts a "Septic Requests for Realtors" form on its septic page, which is the fastest way for an agent to get a copy of the permit before an inspection. The permit shows how many bedrooms the system was designed for, and the layout drawing helps put the tank where it belongs instead of where someone remembers it. More on Burke County records.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Morganton septic FAQ

Do you inspect septic systems on Lake James?

Yes. Lake James lots on both the Burke and McDowell sides are a regular part of my work. Tell me when you book if the lot is steep or the tank is near the water; it affects how long the inspection takes and what equipment I bring.

Where is the Burke County septic permit office?

Burke County Environmental Health is at 200 Avery Avenue in Morganton, 828-764-9000. Agents can use the county's "Septic Requests for Realtors" form to ask for a copy of an existing permit or layout.

Can you do the home inspection the same day?

Yes, through my other company, Inspect-All Home Inspections. Home inspection, septic inspection, radon and well water testing can all be scheduled for one visit.
