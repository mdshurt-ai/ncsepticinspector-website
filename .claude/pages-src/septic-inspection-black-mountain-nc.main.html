Systems older than the paperwork

Buncombe County didn't issue septic permits before 1977, and the early permits were kept on carbon paper until they were digitized in 2007. About four in ten homes in Black Mountain were built before 1980 and roughly one in ten before 1940, so a meaningful share of the systems here have no county record at all. That isn't a defect by itself, but it means the inspection has to establish everything on site: where the tank is, what it's made of, what condition it's in, and whether the drain field is doing its job.

At about 2,300 feet in the Swannanoa Valley, the lots run from valley floor to steep hillside, and the systems follow. Hillside homes often have pump systems or fields benched into the slope, with more components to evaluate than a flat-lot gravity system.

After Helene: Flat Creek, the river and the slopes

The National Weather Service's Helene summary describes historic flooding in Buncombe County; in Black Mountain, Flat Creek Road washed out, and news reports counted hundreds of landslides across the county. On a lot that flooded or slid, the septic questions are whether the tank settled or shifted, whether lines were cut, whether silt filled the tank, and whether effluent is surfacing. I look for all of it and report what limited the inspection, if anything did.

Buncombe County septic records

Buncombe's Environmental Health office (35 Woodfin St., Asheville, permitting office 828-250-5360) puts well and septic records in the county's online development portal, searchable without registering. The county's own search guide says to try the parcel number first, then the address, then the street name with a wildcard, and that the "OP" attachment on a septic record is the final drawing. Well permitting began in 2005, so an older well has no record either. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Black Mountain septic FAQ

The county has no septic permit for the house. Is that a problem?

Not by itself. Buncombe County didn't issue septic permits before 1977, so older systems simply predate the files. The inspection establishes the system's location and condition on site, and the report states that no record was available.

Do you cover Swannanoa, Montreat and Ridgecrest?

Yes. Eastern Buncombe County, from Swannanoa through Black Mountain to Ridgecrest and Montreat, is part of my service area. Steep access is normal; tell me what to expect.

Can I search Buncombe County septic records myself?

Yes, on the county's online development portal, without registering. Search by parcel number first; older parcel numbers sometimes find older permits. The county posts a search guide that explains the tricks.
