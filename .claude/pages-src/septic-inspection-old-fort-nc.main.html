Septic Inspections in Old Fort, NC

Old Fort was one of the hardest-hit towns in Western North Carolina during Helene, and the ground around it still carries the story. Outside the town's lines, properties run on their own well and septic, and those are the systems I inspect.

Septic outside the town's lines

The Town of Old Fort runs its own water and sewer system, and Helene submerged the sewer plant under fourteen feet of water and left the town with recurring line breaks for months. Beyond the town system, though, properties toward Pisgah, up the Old Fort Mountain and along the county roads have their own septic systems, and those are the ones a point-of-sale inspection covers. Census estimates for a town this small carry wide margins, but they put the median home around 1965 and roughly a quarter of homes as mobile homes, two housing types with their own septic patterns: no permit on file for the first, a system sized for the original unit for the second.

Flooded lots along Mill Creek and the Catawba

On September 27, 2024, Mill Creek flooded the town and met the Catawba River, leaving downtown under several feet of water and a foot of mud. For a septic system, that kind of event can settle the tank, shift the lines, fill the tank with silt and drown the drain field. NCDHHS's post-Helene guidance was to have flooded systems inspected and to watch for soil settling over the tank or field, parts that moved or surfaced, and sewage backing up or standing on the ground. Those are the first things I check on a lot that flooded, and if the house was repaired afterward, any paperwork on the septic work is worth having next to the report.

McDowell County septic records

Old Fort's septic permits are with the Foothills Health District, which runs McDowell County's environmental health program and an online Septic Records Portal with permits and layouts where they exist. The McDowell office is at 408 Spaulding Rd. in Marion, 828-652-6811. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Old Fort septic FAQ

The property was repaired after the flood. Does that change the inspection?

It changes what I pay attention to. I look for settling over the tank and field, shifted or surfaced components, silt in the tank, and effluent on the ground, and the report describes what I can see on inspection day. It doesn't confirm that repairs were permitted; the health district's repair permit file does that.

Do you inspect septic systems for mobile and manufactured homes?

Yes. Manufactured-home systems are common around Old Fort. The permit's bedroom count and the home as it stands today, including any additions, are the first comparison the state's inspection rule calls for.

Where do I find Old Fort septic permits?

Through the Foothills Health District's Septic Records Portal, or by calling the McDowell office in Marion at 828-652-6811.
