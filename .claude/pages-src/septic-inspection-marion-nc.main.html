Septic Inspections in Marion, NC

Marion sits where the foothills meet the Blue Ridge, and most of the county around it runs on private wells and septic systems. I inspect septic from East Marion's mill village to Nebo, Lake James and the Lake Tahoma side of the county.

Septic and wells are the norm here

The City of Marion runs its own water system and the county system is small, so once you're past the city lines, a property almost always has its own well and its own septic system. Marion's housing is among the oldest in the area, with a median year built of 1968 and more than six in ten homes built before 1980, and about one in four homes countywide is a mobile home. Older systems and manufactured-home systems each have their own patterns: the first often has no permit and a tank that's never been opened, the second often has a system sized for the original unit and not the additions that followed.

Helene in McDowell County

The National Weather Service called Helene's flooding in McDowell County historic, likely the most severe in the county's recorded history, with Buck Creek, the Catawba River, Goose Creek, Lake James and Lake Tahoma all out of their banks. The county reported that calls to its disaster hotline afterward were mostly water, well and septic issues. If the property sat in water or had repairs after the storm, the septic system belongs on the inspection list: settling over the tank or field, components that moved or surfaced, and sewage backing up are the signs NCDHHS told owners to watch for, and they're the first things I look for on a flooded lot.

McDowell County septic records

McDowell's environmental health program is run by the Foothills Health District, whose online Septic Records Portal holds inspection details, permit information and system layouts where they exist. Read the district's search instructions before you start; finding the right property record is the hard part. The McDowell office is at 408 Spaulding Rd. in Marion, 828-652-6811. Records guide.

What the inspection covers

Every inspection follows the state's minimum point-of-sale standard (21 NCAC 39 .1006) and the same order: compare the advertised bedroom count with the permit, locate and mark the tank, uncover and open it, evaluate the tank and its components, evaluate the pump tank and control panel if there is one, check the drain field area, and put it in writing with photos. If you want the tank pumped, I'll arrange a pumping company for the same visit. The full process, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

Marion septic FAQ

Do you inspect septic systems at Lake James and Lake Tahoma?

Yes, on the McDowell side as well as the Burke side of Lake James. Lake lots often mean steep grades and tight setbacks from the water; mention it when you book.

The property flooded in Helene. What will you look for?

Settling or sinkholes over the tank and drain field, parts that moved or came to the surface, silt in the tank, effluent on the ground, and anything that limited the inspection. If the system needs more than an inspection, the report refers you to the health district or a certified contractor.

Where are McDowell County septic permits kept?

At the Foothills Health District, which runs an online Septic Records Portal for McDowell and Rutherford counties. The Marion office is at 408 Spaulding Rd., 828-652-6811.
