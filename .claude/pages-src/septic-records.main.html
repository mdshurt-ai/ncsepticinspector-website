How to Find the Septic Permit, County by County

Every county in my service area keeps its own septic permits and layout drawings, and every one of them does it differently. This is where to look, what to ask for, and what the record will and won't tell you.

Why the permit is worth twenty minutes

A septic permit, and the layout drawing that often comes with it, shows where the system was supposed to go, what kind of system it is, and how many bedrooms it was designed for. That last number matters more than any other line on the page: North Carolina sizes septic systems by bedroom count, and the state's point-of-sale inspection rule starts by comparing the permit's bedroom count with the number the house is advertised with (21 NCAC 39 .1006). A mismatch is a conversation you want to have at the start of a transaction, not the end.

The record also saves time on inspection day. A layout drawing can put the tank within a few feet; without one, locating it is the first job. What the permit can't tell you is the condition of the tank today. That's what the inspection is for.

What you'll need

  • The property address, and the parcel number (PIN) from the county tax site. Several counties search by parcel first, and parcel numbers change when land is split or combined, so an older number sometimes finds an older permit.
  • The owner's name as it appeared when the system was installed, for the counties that search that way.
  • Patience with older systems. Permits from the 1970s and 1980s may be a card in a file drawer, a scanned page with a hand drawing, or nothing at all.

Burke County (Morganton, Valdese, Connelly Springs, Glen Alpine)

Burke County Environmental Health, 200 Avery Avenue, Morganton, 828-764-9000. The county posts a "Septic Requests for Realtors" form and an "Authorization to Act as an Agent" form on its septic page, which is the cleanest way for an agent to request a copy of an existing permit or layout. The office also handles existing-system verification, the process for confirming what a system can support when a bedroom or structure is being added.

Catawba County (Hickory, Newton, Conover, Maiden)

Catawba County Environmental Health takes requests for a copy of an existing septic permit through an online form on its Environmental Health forms page. Email EHAdmin@catawbacountync.gov, or call the county at 828-465-8200 (25 Government Drive, Newton). Newer applications are in the county's ePermit system.

Caldwell County (Lenoir, Hudson, Granite Falls)

Caldwell County Environmental Health, 2345 Morganton Blvd., Lenoir, 828-426-8579, envhealth@caldwellcountync.org. Use the Septic System Record Request form posted on the county's septic and wells page.

McDowell County (Marion, Old Fort, Nebo)

McDowell's environmental health program is run by the Foothills Health District, which has an online Septic Records Portal with inspection details, permit information and system layouts when they exist. Read the district's search instructions first; finding the right property record is the hard part. The McDowell office is at 408 Spaulding Rd., Marion, 828-652-6811.

Buncombe County (Black Mountain, Swannanoa, Fairview)

Buncombe's Environmental Health office (35 Woodfin St., Asheville, permitting office 828-250-5360) puts well and septic records in the county's online development portal, searchable without registering. The county's own search guide is worth reading: try the parcel number first, then the address, then the street name with a wildcard. Two dates to know: Buncombe didn't issue septic permits before 1977, and well permitting began in 2005, so older systems may simply have no record. When you find a septic record, the "OP" attachment is the final drawing.

Watauga County (Boone, Blowing Rock, Valle Crucis)

AppHealthCare, the district health department for Watauga, Ashe and Alleghany counties, offers an online septic system permit search from its Environmental Health page. The Watauga office number listed for environmental health questions is 828-264-4995.

Alexander County (Taylorsville, Bethlehem, Stony Point)

Alexander County Health Department, Environmental Health, 151 W Main Ave., Taylorsville, 828-632-1000. There's no online search; call or visit to ask for a copy of the permit and any layout on file. The office also evaluates existing systems for additions and replacements.

Lincoln County (the Lincoln County side of Maiden)

Lincoln County Environmental Health, 353 N. Generals Blvd., Lincolnton, has a "Request Septic or Well Permit" option; include the property address or parcel number. The county's public records request link is on the same page.

Office details change. Addresses, phone numbers and forms above were checked against each county's own site in October 2026. If a link has moved, the county's environmental health office is still the place to ask, and I'm glad to help you find it.

Reading what you find

  • Improvement permit / authorization to construct: the approval to install, with the site evaluation. Shows the design, not what was built.
  • Operation permit: issued after the final inspection. This is the one that states the system as installed and the bedroom count. In Buncombe's portal it's the "OP" file; in other counties ask for the operation permit and the final layout.
  • Repair permit: a later repair or replacement, often with a new drawing. Worth having, because the current tank may not be where the original drawing shows.
  • No record: common for systems older than the county's files. It isn't a defect by itself, but it means the inspection has to establish everything from the ground up.

Whatever the record says, it describes a day in the past. The condition of the tank, the tees, the drain field and the pump system today is what a point-of-sale inspection establishes.
