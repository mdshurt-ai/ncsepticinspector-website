Why an independent inspector

Nothing to sell you but the inspection

Most septic inspections in this part of the state are done by companies that also pump, grade or repair. I only inspect, and I'm certified by the state to do it.

No pumping, no repairs, no conflict

A finding in my report never turns into a repair invoice from me. If something needs work, the report points you to the county health department or a certified contractor, as the state rules require.

Certified for the sale

North Carolina requires anyone who inspects a septic system for a sale, refinance or title transfer to be certified by the NC On-Site Wastewater Contractors and Inspectors Certification Board (N.C.G.S. 90A-72). I'm Inspector #6007I, and my inspections follow the board's rules for what gets checked and reported.

Knows the real estate timeline

As a licensed NC real estate broker, I'm extremely knowledgeable about the real estate process and its timelines. Digging, county records and a contractor follow-up all take time, and I plan around your due diligence date.

How it works

Five steps, start to finish

The job follows the same order on every property. The full version, with photos of each step, is on the septic inspection page.

  1. 1

    Locate

    County permit or layout drawing when one exists, what the owner knows, then the tank is found and marked on site.

  2. 2

    Uncover & open

    Buried lids are dug up, by hand or with a mini excavator, and removed. Expect some digging in the yard.

  3. 3

    Evaluate the tank

    Lid, walls, inlet and outlet, tees and filter, water level, roots, leakage, how water flows in, and the solids level.

  4. 4

    Drain field

    The dispersal field is checked for surfacing, wet spots, traffic, vegetation and drainage, and whether effluent is reaching it.

  5. 5

    Written report

    What I found, in plain language with photos, and what it means for the next step. Delivered promptly, inside the state's 10-business-day requirement.

Before the inspection

Find the septic permit first

Each county keeps its own septic permits and layout drawings, and how far back they go varies. A permit shows where the system was supposed to go and how many bedrooms it was designed for. It doesn't show the condition of the tank today, which is what the inspection is for.
